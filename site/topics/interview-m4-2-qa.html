M4.2 Question Bank: Severity vs Priority (35 Questions)

Tier 1: Core Definitions & Examples (Q1–Q10)

  1. Q1: Define Severity vs Priority in one crisp sentence.
    A: Severity measures the technical impact of a bug on the system (assigned by QA), while Priority measures the business urgency of fixing it (assigned by Product/Lead).
  2. Q2: Give a real example of High Severity & High Priority.
    A: Checkout payment fails with HTTP 500 for all customers, or Login crashes the application.
  3. Q3: Give a real example of High Severity & Low Priority.
    A: Application crashes on an obsolete/hidden admin export page used once a year on a legacy browser.
  4. Q4: Give a real example of Low Severity & High Priority.
    A: Misspelled company logo/brand name on the homepage, wrong customer care number, or wrong copyright year right before a press launch.
  5. Q5: Give a real example of Low Severity & Low Priority.
    A: Minor spelling typo or 2px alignment shift inside an obscure Terms & Conditions sub-page.
  6. Q6: Who sets Severity and who sets Priority?
    A: QA sets Severity based on technical impact; Product Owner / Dev Lead / Bug Triage sets Priority based on release timelines and business ROI.
  7. Q7: What is a "Showstopper / Blocker" defect?
    A: A defect that completely halts further testing or prevents users from entering the core workflow (e.g., server fails to start or Login fails).
  8. Q8: What makes a bug "Critical" vs "Major"?
    A: Critical breaks a major function with no workaround; Major degrades a function where a temporary workaround exists.
  9. Q9: Does a High Severity bug always get High Priority?
    A: No—as shown by High Severity / Low Priority bugs on rarely used features or features scheduled for deprecation.
  10. Q10: Can QA suggest Priority when logging a bug?
    A: Yes, QA can propose an initial Priority, but Product/Triage has final authority to adjust it based on sprint scope.

Tier 2: Why / Why Not & Triage Dynamics (Q11–Q18)

  1. Q11: Can the Severity of a bug change over time, or does only Priority change?
    A: Priority changes frequently (e.g., a Low Priority typo becomes Urgent P1 the night before a CEO demo). Severity is technical and usually stays fixed unless new analysis reveals the crash affects far more data than initially thought.
  2. Q12: Why does an incorrect product price display (`$10` instead of `$1,000`) classify as High Severity AND High Priority?
    A: Even without a system crash, incorrect financial calculation causes direct revenue loss and legal liability.
  3. Q13: How do you decide Severity when a bug only occurs on 1 specific mobile viewport (320px iPhone SE)?
    A: Check analytics: if 25% of users are on small viewports and the "Buy" button is off-screen, it is Critical/High; if 0.05% use it and landscape mode works, Severity is Major/Low Priority.
  4. Q14: Why shouldn't QA mark EVERY bug as Blocker/P1?
    A: "Priority inflation" destroys QA credibility—when everything is P1, nothing is P1, and developers ignore real showstoppers.
  5. Q15: What happens in Bug Triage when Dev says a bug is Minor but QA says it is Critical?
    A: Review against objective rules: Is data corrupted? Is a P0 user flow blocked without a workaround? Does it affect >10% of users? Let impact data settle it.
  6. Q16: How does Severity map to Release Exit Criteria?
    A: Standard Exit Criteria require 0 Open Blocker (S1) and 0 Open Critical (S2) defects, while Minor (S4) defects may be deferred/waived with PO sign-off.
  7. Q17: What is "Severity Creep" vs "Priority Escalation"?
    A: Priority Escalation happens legitimately when business deadlines approach or VIP customers hit a bug.
  8. Q18: In Agile Scrum, when are P1 vs P3 bugs fixed?
    A: P1 bugs are pulled into the active Sprint immediately; P3/P4 bugs go into the Product Backlog for future prioritization.

Tier 3–5: Scenarios, SDET & Rapid-Fire (Q19–Q35)

  1. Q19: Scenario: A security flaw exposes user JWT tokens in URL query strings (`?token=xyz`), but the feature works visually. What is its Severity?
    A: Critical / Blocker (S1/S2) and P1 Urgent—security/privacy breaches are always highest severity regardless of UI appearance.
  2. Q20: Scenario: A CSV export of 500,000 rows times out after 60 seconds, while 1,000-row exports succeed. How do you classify it?
    A: Major (S3) (workaround: export in smaller date ranges) and P2/P3 depending on whether enterprise clients export 500k rows daily.
  3. Q21: Scenario: A dark-mode CSS bug makes white text appear on a white background on the Checkout page. Severity & Priority?
    A: Looks "cosmetic", but functionally blocks 100% of dark-mode users from reading Checkout! Therefore High Severity (Critical) + High Priority (P1/P2).
  4. Q22: Scenario: Automated Selenium Smoke test fails because an element ID changed from `btn-pay` to `pay-now`, while the button still works for humans. Is that a product bug?
    A: No—that is a Test Script Maintenance / Locator issue, not an application defect (though teams should enforce `data-testid` contracts).
  5. Q23: How do you tag Severity in Allure Reports in Java/TestNG?
    A: Use `@Severity(SeverityLevel.CRITICAL)` or `@Severity(SeverityLevel.BLOCKER)` so failed tests automatically highlight business risk in the CI dashboard.
  6. Q24: Which metric tracks severity-weighted quality of a release?
    A: Weighted Defect Density (assigning weights like Blocker=10, Critical=5, Major=2, Minor=1).
  7. Q25: What is the difference between a P0 Test Case and a P1 Defect?
    A: P0 Test Cases cover the most critical app flows; a P1 Defect is an urgent bug that must be fixed immediately.
  8. Q26–Q35 Quick Traps: (Q26) Who sets Severity? QA. (Q27) Who sets Priority? PO/Lead. (Q28) Logo typo = Low Sev / High Pri. (Q29) Rarely used page crash = High Sev / Low Pri. (Q30) Can Priority change without code changes? Yes. (Q31) Can a cosmetic CSS bug be High Severity? Yes, if it hides text or blocks clicks. (Q32) Which defects block Release Sign-Off? Open S1/S2. (Q33) Does Severity depend on release deadline? No (Priority does). (Q34) Does Priority depend on technical stack? No, business urgency. (Q35) What is P4? Lowest urgency, deferred to backlog.
