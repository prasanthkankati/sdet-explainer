M5.2 Question Bank: User Stories, INVEST, DoR vs DoD & Kanban (35 Questions)

Tier 1–5 Complete Question Bank (Q1–Q35)

  1. Q1: What does INVEST stand for in Agile User Stories?
    A: **I**ndependent, **N**egotiable, **V**aluable, **E**stimable, **S**mall, **T**estable.
  2. Q2: What is the difference between Acceptance Criteria and Definition of Done (DoD)?
    A: **Acceptance Criteria** are story-specific behavioral rules (e.g., *"Coupon SAVE20 deducts 20%"*). **DoD** is a team-wide engineering quality checklist applied to *every* story (Unit tests, Code Review, QA Passed, Automation merged).
  3. Q3: What is the difference between DoR and DoD?
    A: **DoR (Definition of Ready)** checks if a story is ready to *start* in Sprint Planning; **DoD (Definition of Done)** checks if the finished story is ready to *ship*.
  4. Q4: What are Story Points and why use Fibonacci (`1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13`) instead of Hours?
    A: Story Points measure relative **Complexity + Effort + Risk**, not clock hours (a 5-point story takes a senior dev 4h and a junior dev 12h, yet its complexity is still 5). Fibonacci reflects increasing uncertainty as stories grow larger.
  5. Q5: What is Team Velocity and can you compare Team A's velocity (`40`) with Team B's velocity (`25`)?
    A: Velocity = average Story Points of `Done` stories per Sprint. You **NEVER** compare velocities across teams because Story Point baselines are relative to each team.
  6. Q6: What is the difference between a Sprint Burndown Chart and a Burnup Chart?
    A: **Burndown** shows remaining work trending down toward 0; **Burnup** shows completed work trending upward alongside a total-scope line (making scope creep visible immediately).
  7. Q7: What is Scrum vs Kanban?
    A: **Scrum** uses fixed-length Sprints, prescribed roles, and Velocity. **Kanban** uses continuous flow on a board with strict **WIP (Work-In-Progress) Limits**, measuring **Lead Time** and **Cycle Time**.
  8. Q8: What is the difference between Lead Time and Cycle Time in Kanban/Jira?
    A: **Lead Time** = total time from ticket creation in Backlog until delivery to Customer. **Cycle Time** = active engineering time from `In Progress` until `Done`.
  9. Q9: What is the difference between Epic, Story, Task, and Sub-task in Jira?
    A: **Epic** (large multi-sprint initiative, e.g., *"Checkout Redesign"*) → **User Story** (deliverable user value in 1 Sprint) → **Task/Sub-task** (technical breakdown like *"Write Selenium Test"* or *"Create DB Table"*).
  10. Q10: Should QA log a sub-task bug found inside an active Sprint Story as a separate Jira Bug or fix it inside the Story?
    A: Defects found inside an *in-progress Sprint Story* before the story is `Done` are usually logged as a **Bug Sub-task** under that Story (with 0 extra Story Points!) so the Story cannot close until fixed.
  11. Q11–Q35 Quick Drills: (Q11) Should a bug fix get Story Points? **No for in-sprint story bugs; yes/time-boxed for escaped production bugs**. (Q12) What is Planning Poker? **Consensus-based Story Point estimation**. (Q13) What is T-Shirt Sizing (`S, M, L, XL`)? **High-level Epic estimation**. (Q14) What is a WIP Limit? **Max tickets allowed in a column like `In QA (Max 3)` to prevent bottlenecks**. (Q15) What is Escaped Defect Rate in Sprint metrics? **Prod bugs / Sprint** (target 0). (Q16–Q35: Writing Gherkin `Given-When-Then` for Boundary Values, Handling `13`-point Epics by splitting vertically [not Frontend story + Backend story], Jira Workflows [`To Do -> In Progress -> Code Review -> Ready for QA -> In QA -> Done`], QA Capacity calculation when 1 QA is on leave, and why `Automated Test Coverage` belongs in DoD).
